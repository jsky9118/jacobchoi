Why this topic

Jacob swims year-round and works as a lifeguard, and swimmer’s ear is the infection people at the pool talk about most. He expected to write about pool water. The studies led somewhere else: toward diabetes, older patients, and the difference between fungal and bacterial infection.

Main findings

  • Two diseases share one name. Otomycosis was reported at every age, was treated in a clinic, and improved in 83% to 92% of patients. Necrotizing otitis externa was reported almost only in older adults and needed a mean of 18 to 29 weeks of treatment.
  • The patient mattered more than the organism. Aspergillus caused mild infection in healthy adults and invasive disease in older patients. About 74% of patients with necrotizing otitis externa had diabetes in the five studies that reported it.
  • Fungal and bacterial infection must be told apart. Antibacterial drops do not treat fungi, and fungi were found in 29% to 34% of necrotizing cases in two series.
  • For swimmers, time in the water mattered and water quality did not. Swimming more than four hours a week raised the risk 4.8 times in the one included study of swimmers, which dates from 1982. No study since 2020 examined swimmers, which is the gap the paper names.
